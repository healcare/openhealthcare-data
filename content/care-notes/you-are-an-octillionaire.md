## The science beneath the poem

**How many electrons?** An octillion means 10²⁷ here—a one followed by 27 zeros. There is no single electron count for every person; mass and composition matter. To illustrate the scale, a hypothetical 70 kg quantity of water contains about 2.3 × 10²⁸ electrons—roughly **23 octillion**. That calculation uses about 18 grams per mole of water, 10 electrons per neutral water molecule, and Avogadro’s constant. It is an illustrative calculation, not a measured human-body count; a body is not entirely water. We therefore use “octillions” rather than claiming exactly seven. [1,4]

**Cellular batteries.** Pumps maintain ion concentration differences, while selective membrane permeability helps establish voltage. Around **−70 millivolts** is a familiar example for a resting neuron, not a universal value for every cell or moment. Nerve and muscle electrical signals involve moving ions, rather than electrons flowing through a wire. [2]

**The ATP factory.** In mitochondria, electron transfers in the respiratory chain help pump protons across the inner membrane. Their return through ATP synthase drives ATP production. This is a way cells convert chemical energy; simply possessing electrons does not provide an unlimited energy supply. [3]

**Continuous chemistry.** Electrons participate in the bonds and reactions of biological molecules, including DNA and proteins. Many remain associated with atoms and stable bonds; not every electron is continually being passed along a metabolic pathway. The body is approximately electrically neutral overall. [1]
