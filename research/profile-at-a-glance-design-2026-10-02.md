# Profile at-a-glance design direction

October 2, 2026. Based on user-provided screenshots of a Google entity overview and the OpenHealthcare Anna Guanche profile. These screenshots are design references, not evidence for additional clinician facts.

Problem: Current Guanche header devotes a large empty left column to name, generic Practice profile text and research limitations. The patient cannot readily identify specialty, care focus or location without scrolling. The reference uses compact label/value tiles, a clear identity line, varied photo sizes and a readable overview to make information scannable.

Authorized direction: preserve existing structure, galleries, sourced narrative, soft colors, canonical URLs and review states. Introduce a compact At a glance group near name and portrait. Prefer three or four useful facts, with short labels and prominent values. Choose from specialty/clinician role, sourced care focus, exact practice city/location, clinician languages with proficiency qualifiers, practice-stated insurance, or actual intake/availability. Choose the most relevant supported information per clinician. Do not fill a fixed quota with invented or irrelevant facts. Unknown important access criteria can be plainly identified as unknown.

A brief overview should describe who the clinician is and their sourced focus in one or two sentences. One optional interesting career or personal detail may appear when directly supported and useful; no celebrity associations, promotional superiority, quality ranking, personal speculation or unsupported credentials. Do not copy the reference's age/spouse focus into healthcare profiles.

Separate specialty, care focus and language facts from licensing verification, insurance enrollment and current capacity. Board certification must be specifically attributed where not independently checked. Existing source facts may be reused; no new claim may be generated from a design example. Anna Guanche's named Calabasas-vs-Beverly-Hills location conflict must remain explicit; do not silently relabel her as Beverly Hills.

Visual hierarchy: name and concrete role first; compact brief overview and facts next; restrained portrait alongside. Pastel tiles, consistent spacing, readable contrast and clear label/value typography. Avoid decorative empty cards, giant whitespace, repetitive full-width notices or extra navigation burden. Source links stay easy to reach with a concise checked-date/evidence line. Material warnings such as temporary closure or conflicting location remain visible above the fold. Routine provenance may be less visually dominant without being hidden or weakened.

Small screens: preserve reading order and responsive compact tiles without horizontal overflow. Color never carries status alone. Fact/source interactions must be keyboard accessible. Keep directory-image click behavior leading to the OHC profile and separate photo-source links. Preserve portrait attribution, image review constraints and heart fallbacks.

Verify with Anna Guanche, an ordinary complete profile, Payam's temporarily closed practice and a sparse profile. Show honest partial data rather than padding. Preview only: no deployment or automatic clearance of holds.
